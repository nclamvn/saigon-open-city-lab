Copernicus DEM - Global and European Digital Elevation Model The Copernicus DEM is a Digital Surface Model (DSM) that represents the surface of the Earth including buildings, infrastructure and vegetation. The Copernicus DEM is provided in 3 different instances: EEA-10, GLO-30 and GLO-90. Data were acquired by the TanDEM-X mission between 2011 and 2015. If any Copernicus DEM products show a sensing date earlier than 2011, this is because older elevation models were used to fill data gaps. To make sure you find all available data in the Copernicus Browser, it is recommended to extend the search time range back to 2001. The datasets were made available for use in 2019 and will be maintained until 2026. All registered users (i.e. How to register to access Copernicus Contributing Missions data ) can freely access the 30m and 90m resolution instances of the Copernicus DEM. Access to the 10m resolution instances over Europe is limited to a specific subset of users, as outlined in the ESA-User license for the use of CCM data and CCM Data Access Restrictions . The Copernicus DEM is a Digital Surface Model (DSM) which represents the top-reflective surface of the Earth including buildings, infrastructure and vegetation. This DEM is derived from an edited DSM named WorldDEMTM , i.e. flattening of water bodies and consistent flow of rivers has been included. Editing of shore- and coastlines, special features such as airports and implausible terrain structures has also been applied. In contrast to Digital Surface Models such as Copernicus DEM, a Digital Terrain Model (DTM) represents the Earth's terrain with buildings, infrastructure and vegetation being removed in a DEM editing process. Additionally, hybrid DEMs are possible with e. g. only vegetation or only buildings / infrastructure removed. Further information on DEM types is available in Peter Guth et.al.: Digital Elevation Models: Terminology and Definitions, 2021. ( https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/18/3581 ) The aforementioned differentiation should be considered when comparing DSMs, such as the current Copernicus DEM, with external reference data (e.g. LiDAR-based DTM data). EEA-10: European coverage EEA-10 covers 39 European countries including all islands of those countries plus French Overseas Departments (excluding French Overseas Territories) at a resolution of 10 metres with a surface coverage of ~ 6 M km 2 . Land surface coverage within Copernicus DEM – EEA-10 GLO-30 and GLO-90: worldwide coverage GLO-30 offers global coverage at a resolution of 30 metres. GLO-90 offers global coverage at a resolution of 90 metres. Both datasets have a surface coverage of ~ 149 M km 2 . Land surface coverage within Copernicus DEM – GLO-30 & GLO-90 Nine releases are currently available for all Copernicus DEM instances. See in “Releases” section of this page for more details. With the addition of AM2 and AMP, the Copernicus DEM is further enhanced for several applicators such as: Water body mapping Land surface classification Environmental monitoring Both layers are available in the CDSE catalogue under the dataset COP-DEM_AMP-12-DGED, and are available for search and download via both the OData and S3 interfaces. For example, the following OData query performs a search on these products: catalogue.dataspace.copernicus.eu/odata/v1/Products?$filter=Attributes/OData.CSC.StringAttribute/any(att:att/Name eq %27datasetFull%27 and att/OData.CSC.StringAttribute/Value eq %27COP-DEM_AMP-12-DGED%27)&$expand=Attributes These new data are available for download to users belonging to the following categories: Copernicus Services and Union Institutional Users Union Research Projects National Public Authorities Copernicus Operators. Acceptance of the CCM user license is required, allowing the two layers to be reused by other eligible user groups.
