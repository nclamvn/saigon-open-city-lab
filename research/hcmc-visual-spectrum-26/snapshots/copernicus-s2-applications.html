S2 Applications

Overview of Sentinel-2 Applications

Sentinel-2 is a European mission equipped with a wide-swath Multispectral Instrument (MSI) to ensure a high-revisit frequency and provide high-resolution imagery to support the Copernicus programme.

Sentinel-2 data products are made available systematically and free of charge to all users, providing data for the following priority service areas.

To start using the data visit CDSE browser and CDSE videos.

CLMS: Copernicus Land Monitoring Service

The Copernicus Land Monitoring Service (CLMS) became operational in 2012 and provides geographical information on land cover and its changes, land use, ground motion, vegetation state, water cycle, and Earth’s surface energy variables to a broad range of users in Europe and across the world. The CLMS supports policy and decision-making processes in the frame of the European Green Deal and the UN Conventions and Sustainable Development Goals. It comprises:

  • A Pan-European component, coordinated by the European Environment Agency (EEA)

  • A global component coordinated by the European Commission DG Joint Research Centre (JRC)

The Sentinel-2 mission provides accurate radiometric data and a high level of detail, which is fundamental to CLMS and, with its twin-satellite capability, ensures frequent and systematic coverage to support state-of-the-art mapping of land cover and its dynamics, and an accurate assessment of bio- and geophysical parameters. To this end, Sentinel-2 enables CLMS to support a variety of application areas including:

  • Spatial planning and urban studies

  • Agro-environmental and crop monitoring

  • Food security

  • Water management

  • Forest and vegetation monitoring

  • Land carbon and natural resource assessments

  • Ecosystem accounting and assessment

  • Mitigation/adaptation to climate change

The CLMS makes use of Sentinel-2 data in the following activities and and services.

Sentinel-2 Global Mosaic

The Sentinel-2 Global Mosaic (S2GM) provides Sentinel-2 surface reflectance time series composites in three spatial resolutions and from different compositing periods ranging from one day to one year. Users can tailor mosaics to their specific requirements in terms of area of interest, compositing period, included bands, file format, and metadata content.

Land Cover Forest Mapping

The Land Cover and Tropical Forest Mapping and Monitoring activity (LCFM) provides a high-resolution Sentinel-2 based, dynamic global land cover service. By generating frequent, sub-annual land surface features and land cover categories it will consolidate these into global annual land cover maps and tropical forest monitoring products at 10 m resolution.

Hot Spot Monitoring

The Hot Spot Monitoring (HSM) activity provides on demand detailed land cover and land cover change information on specific areas of interest outside the European Union. This Copernicus Service supports field projects and policies developed by the EU in the framework of EU international policies.

European Land Cover and Land Use Models

CORINE Land Cover (CLC) and CLCplus are CLMS’ flagship land cover and land use models of Europe (i.e. 38 EEA member and cooperating countries and the United Kingdom). CLC offers rich thematic detail (44 thematic land cover and land use classes) and an outstanding and consistent record starting from 1990. The CLCplus product suite provides a full land cover portfolio from a baseline, high-resolution product (CLCplus Backbone; cf. Figure 1) to tailored expert products (CLCplus Instances) that involve multiple data sources and aim to meet the requirements of specific (policy) use cases. Sentinel-2 data are the basis for the latest update of CLC (2024) and the biennial CLCplus Backbone products.

High-Resolution Layers

The concept of High-Resolution Layers (HRL) dates back to a pre-Sentinel era. Since then, the HRLs have evolved to specialised products dedicated to specific land surface characteristics across Europe. For instance, HRLs that apply Sentinel-2 time series monitor the dynamic nature of sealed and built-up areas (HRL Imperviousness) and tree cover (HRL Forests), or map crop types and agricultural practices (HRL Croplands) as well as grasslands and mowing events (HRL Grasslands) on an annual basis.

Bio and geophysical parameters

CLMS’ bio and geophysical parameters consist of a suite of qualified products that allow for the quantitative and systematic monitoring of the status and dynamics of the land surface. The products in many cases offer near-real time and aggregated products (e.g. seasonal/annual trajectories and phenology) that facilitate the timeliness and level of detail requested for different applications. The operational products relying on Sentinel-2 are used to monitor vegetation properties and dynamics (e.g. Fraction of Vegetation Cover, Fraction of Absorbed Photosynthetically Active Radiation, Leaf Area Index), the cryosphere (e.g. Snow Cover Extent) as well as Water Bodies at global scale.

Geospatial CLMS products created from Sentinel-2 data directly facilitate, among others, applications such as:

  • Statistical indicators on soil sealing, forest connectivity and landscape fragmentation across Europe

  • Land accounting

  • Urban planning and climate adaptation

  • Land monitoring

  • Infrastructure and water management

  • Conservation and biodiversity monitoring

  • Support to sample selection for large-area, ground-based surveys such as LUCAS

CMEMS: Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service

The Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS) provides regular and systematic reference information on the physical state, variability and dynamics of the ocean and marine ecosystems of both the oceans worldwide and the European seas, taking into account the state of the blue (physical), white (sea ice) and green (biogeochemical) ocean.

CMEMS is implemented by Mercator Ocean International, a global ocean analysis and forecasting centre, under a contributing agreement with the European Commission (EC) in 2014. It provides key inputs that support major EU and international policies and initiatives and can contribute to:

  • Combating pollution

  • Marine protection

  • Maritime safety and routing

  • Sustainable use of ocean resources

  • Developing marine energy resources

  • Blue growth

  • Climate monitoring

  • Weather forecasting

It also aims to increase awareness among the public by providing European and global citizens with information about ocean-related issues.

Geophysical measurements

High resolution (100 m x 100 m) turbidity, Chlorophyll and Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM) products for coastal waters are provided daily; these factors influence light penetration and water composition and therefore impact marine ecosystems. Its total suspended matter and turbidity data are vital for use in downstream applications, especially for coastal areas. An example of the turbidity measured in the North West Shelf area in April 2020 is provided in Figure 2.

Bathymetry, i.e., the determination of water depths to define submarine topography, is a key environmental parameter in coastal zones for a wide variety of coastal applications, and to improve coastal ocean modelling. Starting in 2026, a new product providing Sentinel-2 based global gridded (level-4) satellite-bathymetry will be available on the Copernicus Marine Service catalogue.

Sentinel-2 has proven to be a very important data source for sea ice and iceberg analysis near shore in ice covered waters; the high spatial resolution makes it well suited for validation purposes as well as for fully understanding the ice conditions extracted from Sentinel-1.

CEMS: Copernicus Emergency Management Service

The Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CEMS) provides accurate and timely geospatial information derived from satellite remote sensing to all organisations and entities to facilitate the mitigation and response of many types of disasters or crises including:

  • Natural disasters (floods, fires, landslides, storms, earthquakes, etc.)

  • Industrial accidents

  • Humanitarian crises (for instance after a period of severe drought, famine etc.)

Where available, the satellite data are complemented by in situ or open data sources. The service started operations in April 2012 and is managed by the European Commission DG Joint Research Centre (JRC). The service consists of two main components:

  • The On-Demand Mapping service, which provides geospatial information with worldwide coverage based on satellite imagery. The Rapid Mapping module is dedicated to support the response management of civil protection and rescue services and provides information on the level of damage (such as destroyed buildings in an earthquake zone) and the extent and impact of natural disasters (such as delineation of a flooded area). The Risk & Recovery module provides geospatial data for the pre-event (risk assessment) and post-event (post disaster needs and reconstruction assessment).

  • The early warning and monitoring component of the service provides overviews on ongoing and forecasted floods in Europe up to 10 days in advance, near real time and historical information on forest fires and forest fire regimes in the European, Middle Eastern and North African regions, drought-relevant information and global population exposure data.

Sentinel-2 data are used in 80% of the Rapid Mapping activations. Due to its global and continuous coverage it is one of the main sources of information mostly for understanding the situation before the event and to monitor the evolution of wildfires in forested areas. In the Risk & Recovery module, Sentinel-2’s high quality data are used for land use change detection, for validation of models and many other use cases.

CSS-SESA: Copernicus Service on Support to EU External and Security Actions

The Copernicus Security Service component on Support to EU External and Security Actions (SESA) is a European geospatial information service that assists the EU and its Member States in the framework of applicable Union policies and legislation along the following main policy groups, associated to key responsibilities across EU Institutions and Member States.

The information provided contributes to improving European capacities in crisis prevention, preparedness and response. It also aims to assist in situations of crisis or emerging crisis and supports them in order to prevent global and transregional threats having a destabilising effect.

SESA fulfils user needs for information in a wide range of application areas. Each application area represents critical and relevant topics for the current and upcoming EU policies and interests.

SESA is mostly supported by the Copernicus Contributing Missions both new acquisition and archive. However, the large amount of high-resolution Sentinel-2 freely available data allows approaches for monitoring of large areas and to gain new insights. This approach complements the detailed geospatial information analysis that SESA provides.

Some examples of use case (non-exhaustive) include:

  • Identification of potential areas of conflict between communities sharing resources and project construction monitoring in Climate Security application are

  • Environmental damage monitoring, such as illegal logging activity in Environmental Compliance application area.

  • Monitoring illegal activity, in the Rule of Law application area.

  • Road network status assessment, in the Transport Safety and Security application area.

  • True colour: Product with natural colours that represents Earth as humans would naturally see it

  • False colour: The false colour composite is most commonly used to assess plant density and health, since plants reflect near infrared and green light, while they absorb red. Cities and exposed ground are grey or tan, and water appears blue or black.

  • NDVI: The Normalised Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) is a simple, but effective index for quantifying green vegetation. It is a measure of the state of vegetation health based on how plants reflect light at certain wavelengths.

  • False Colour (urban): This composite is used to visualise urbanized areas more clearly. Vegetation is visible in shades of green, while urbanised areas are represented by white, grey, or purple. Soils, sand, and minerals are shown in a variety of colours. Snow and ice appear as dark blue, and water as black or blue. Flooded areas are very dark blue and almost black. The composite is useful for detecting wildfires and calderas of volcanoes, as they are displayed in shades of red and yellow.

  • Moisture Index: The Normalised Difference Moisture Index (NDMI) is used to determine vegetation water content and monitor droughts

  • SWIR: Short Wave Infrared (SWIR) measurements can help scientists estimate how much water is present in plants and soil, as water absorbs SWIR wavelengths

  • NDWI: The Normalised Difference Water Index (NDWI) is most appropriate for water body mapping

  • NDSI: The Sentinel-2 Normalised Difference Snow Index (NDSI) can be used to differentiate between cloud and snow cover as snow absorbs in the short-wave infrared light, but reflects the visible light, whereas cloud is generally reflective in both wavelengths. Snow cover is represented in bright vivid blue.

  • Scene classification: Scene classification was developed to distinguish between cloudy pixels, clear pixels and water pixels of Sentinel-2 data and is a result of ESA’s Scene classification algorithm for Level-2A products only.
